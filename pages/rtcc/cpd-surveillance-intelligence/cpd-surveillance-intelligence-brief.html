Section 01 — Current Systems

Fusus & the
Cleveland RTCC

Cleveland's Real Time Crime Center currently uses Fusus — an Axon-owned video management and alert aggregation platform — as its primary RTCC interface. Here's what it does and where it falls short.

What Fusus Is
Platform Owner
Fusus by Axon
Fusus is a software platform owned by Axon (formerly TASER International). It aggregates video feeds, LPR data, ShotSpotter alerts, and community cameras onto a map-based interface for RTCC operators.
SAFE SMART CLE
Community Camera Program
Cleveland's SAFE SMART CLE program uses Fusus CORE devices to connect privately-owned business cameras to the RTCC. Over 3,700 business cameras are enrolled. Camera owners retain control — police access only on nearby incident.
The Honest Assessment
⚠ Critical Observation
At its operational core, Fusus functions as an expensive networked video management system with good marketing. The CORE device that connects private cameras is essentially an RTSP bridge — straightforward technology that's been available for years. Most of its functionality exists in mature, lower-cost VMS platforms like Genetec or Milestone.
What Fusus Adds Over Basic VMS
  • Geospatial correlation — cameras appear as map pins tied to incident locations, so operators don't need to know camera IDs
  • Event-driven automation — ShotSpotter triggers auto-surface nearby cameras rather than requiring manual lookup
  • Multi-source aggregation — city cameras, private cameras, LPR, body cams, and drones in one interface
  • Community camera onboarding — the SAFE SMART CLE enrollment model is genuinely well-executed
  • CJIS-compliant storage via Amazon GovCloud with role-based access logging
The Core Problem
Cleveland already runs Motorola PremierOne CAD and APX radios. Fusus sits between these native Motorola systems as middleware — adding latency, failure points, and subscription cost for functionality that CommandCentral Aware provides natively at no additional middleware layer.
Section 02 — Current Systems

ShotSpotter
Integration Architecture

ShotSpotter (now SoundThinking) acoustic gunshot detection integrates with both Fusus and CommandCentral Aware via open API, triggering an automated chain of responses from detection through drone deployment.

Integration Chain
01
Acoustic Detection
ShotSpotter sensors triangulate gunfire in near real-time, providing an exact hit location rather than relying on 911 calls. This uncovers gunfire incidents that historically go unreported.
02
API Alert Push
Geolocated alert pushes via open API into both CommandCentral Aware (CCA map pin) and PremierOne CAD simultaneously. No manual operator step required.
03
Auto Camera Surface
CCA workflow automation rule surfaces all cameras within a configurable radius around the detection point. Operators see live video before a single officer arrives on scene.
04
BRINC Drone Trigger
ShotSpotter alert triggers the nearest BRINC dock via one-click CCA workflow. Drone launches within seconds — arriving before responding officers, providing thermal overwatch of shell casing locations.
05
Multi-Channel Delivery
Alert simultaneously reaches RTCC operators, APX radios of responding officers, in-car MDTs via PremierOne CAD, and district supervisor consoles — creating a shared real-time operational picture.
06
LPR Corridor Activation
Flock LPR readers along likely escape corridors are queued for heightened alert monitoring. Any vehicle captured near the detection point is flagged and searched against hot lists automatically.
Real-World Outcome
On the first operational use of the ShotSpotter-BRINC integration in a comparable city, the drone arrived so quickly that the thermal camera identified shell casing locations even though the shooters had already fled — providing evidence that would have been lost without aerial response.
Section 03 — Current Systems

Flock Safety
LPR Network

Cleveland's Flock Safety deployment represents a major operational asset. Understanding the network scale — and why it cannot be replicated by simply switching vendors — is essential to any platform decision.

90,000
Flock Cameras Nationwide
Deployed across 5,000+ law enforcement agencies in all 50 states. Generating 20 billion+ monthly plate reads. Flock's network is 10x larger than the next closest competitor.
35B
Vigilant Historic Records
Motorola Vigilant's database of historic plate detections across 1,800+ government customers. Deep archive — but Flock generates more monthly reads than Vigilant's entire historic database.
Why the Network Size Matters Operationally
  • Cross-jurisdictional cases — when a carjacking suspect crosses from Cleveland into Euclid, Parma, or Akron, Flock cameras in those cities read the plate and alert Cleveland detectives in real time. Vigilant cannot match this coverage density in Northeast Ohio.
  • Amber Alerts — immediate statewide and national plate flagging the moment an alert is issued. The network depth directly impacts child recovery speed.
  • Auto theft recovery — Cleveland has chronically high vehicle theft rates. Flock hot-list alerts on stolen plates across the regional network are one of the most direct recovery tools available.
  • Pattern analysis — vehicles appearing repeatedly near crime scenes across different precincts before a crime is committed, caught by the network density before officers are even aware.
  • Homicide investigations — tracing a suspect vehicle's movements in the hours before and after an incident across multiple jurisdictions using historic read data.
Critical Finding
Removing Flock cameras would take a genuinely important investigative tool away from CPD — not a redundant one. The capital investment is already sunk, the network is enrolled, and the investigative value of the national network is irreplaceable by any other single vendor. The cameras stay.

Section 07 — Strategy

Flock vs Vigilant
LPR Strategy

Should Cleveland replace its Flock LPR cameras with Motorola/Vigilant hardware to achieve full ecosystem consolidation? The answer is nuanced — and probably not what you'd expect.

Case For Replacing Flock
True Native Integration
Vigilant VehicleManager integrates natively with CCA, PremierOne CAD, and CommandCentral Records — no API bridges. Motorola's L-series camera hardware spans every deployment scenario. Advanced analytics include predictive vehicle movement modeling and Common Plate Analysis that Flock doesn't match at investigative depth.
Case Against Replacing Flock
The Network Gap Is Decisive
Flock's 90,000 cameras generating 20B+ monthly reads vs. Vigilant's 1.5B agency-sharing detections. Cleveland's 2,700+ cameras are sunk capital already deployed and enrolled. The investigative value of Flock's national network for cross-jurisdictional cases cannot be replicated by switching vendors.
The Optimal Strategy
Recommended Approach — Keep Both
Keep all 2,700+ Flock cameras in the ground. Feed Flock detections into Vigilant VehicleManager for investigative analytics. Display all LPR data in CommandCentral Aware via the native Vigilant-CCA integration. Retain Flock's national network access for cross-jurisdictional investigations (configuring data sharing to regional Ohio partners only). Add Vigilant mobile LPR on patrol vehicles (L5M) for mobile scanning feeding the same backend.

Result: Flock's unmatched national network depth plus Vigilant's superior investigative analytics plus native CCA display — at no additional hardware cost.
Data Governance Configuration
  • Configure Flock data sharing to named regional partners only — Cuyahoga County Sheriff, Ohio State Highway Patrol, neighboring municipal departments — not the full national network or federal agencies
  • Vigilant VehicleManager provides agency-defined data retention policies and detailed audit logs aligned to CJIS compliance standards
  • Both platforms are CJIS-compliant — the governance choice is which sharing configuration serves Cleveland's legal obligations and community commitments
  • Publish the data sharing configuration publicly as part of the governance compact — this directly addresses activist concerns without reducing operational capability

Section 09 — Community

Responding to
Surveillance Critics

Activists who oppose surveillance technology but demand faster police response are holding two operationally conflicting positions. The city needs to name that contradiction honestly — then offer a real governance compact, not a dismissal.

The Core Contradiction
The Honest Reality
Faster police response without surveillance technology requires one of three things: more officers, better luck, or accepting that some crimes won't be solved. Cleveland has chronic staffing shortages in CPD. You cannot simultaneously reduce technological force multipliers AND maintain response times with a shrinking patrol force. The surveillance infrastructure exists precisely because there aren't enough officers to physically cover 77 square miles of city.
Where Activists Are Right
Legitimate Concern
Data Governance
The nationwide data sharing models of both Flock and Vigilant are real issues. Vehicle location data can flow to federal agencies including ICE without warrant. Cleveland has the ability to configure exactly who sees its data — and should do so publicly.
Legitimate Concern
Audit Trails
Who accessed which cameras, when, and why should be logged, independently audited, and publicly reported. The answer to surveillance overreach isn't removing cameras — it's building accountability infrastructure around them.
Legitimate Concern
History of Misuse
The history of surveillance abuse by American law enforcement is real and documented. Acknowledging this directly — rather than dismissing it — is the only position that builds durable public trust. The city should say so plainly.
The Message in Plain Language
"We hear the concern about surveillance and we share the belief that police power needs accountability and limits. We're not asking you to trust us blindly — we're asking you to review the governance framework we're putting around these tools and tell us where it falls short. What we cannot do is remove technology that is literally the only reason some crime victims get justice in a city where we don't have enough officers to be everywhere at once. Faster response and zero surveillance aren't both available. We're offering faster response with accountable, transparent, limited surveillance — and we're open to making those limits tighter." — Recommended City of Cleveland Public Statement
What The City Should Never Do
Never tell activists that surveillance tools are harmless or that their concerns are unfounded. That destroys credibility and inflames the debate. The concerns are legitimate — acknowledge them, build real accountability around them, and make the operational case honestly. That is the only position that holds up over time.
Section 10 — Community

Governance
Compact

Rather than fighting activists or capitulating to them, Cleveland should offer a concrete, enforceable governance compact with six specific commitments — each verifiable, each with real teeth.

01
Published Data Retention Limits
Flock footage and LPR reads retained for a defined period (30, 60, or 90 days) with automatic deletion. Publicly documented retention schedule, audited annually and reported to City Council.
02
Civilian Oversight With Real Access
Not a rubber-stamp board — a body with actual authority to pull query logs, review system access records, and publish public quarterly reports on how technology is being used and by whom.
03
Configurable Data Sharing — Published
Cleveland commits publicly to sharing Flock LPR data only with named regional law enforcement partners (Cuyahoga County Sheriff, OSHP, regional municipal departments) — not the full national network or federal immigration enforcement.
04
No Facial Recognition — In Ordinance
Cleveland has already committed to no facial recognition technology. This commitment should be codified in municipal ordinance with enforcement mechanism — not just policy language that can be reversed by the next administration.
05
Quarterly Benefit Reporting
Publish quarterly data on what the technology is actually producing: stolen vehicles recovered, response time improvements, cases closed, crimes deterred at enrolled business locations. Public accountability for outcomes, not just inputs.
06
Sunset & Review Clauses
Every surveillance contract comes before City Council for renewal with a mandatory public performance review. The community retains the ability to course-correct. No contract auto-renews without public hearing.
What This Compact Accomplishes
  • Addresses the most legitimate activist concerns with enforceable commitments — not just promises
  • Creates a public accountability record that builds trust over time as outcomes are documented
  • Retains full operational capability of the surveillance infrastructure — no tools are removed
  • Protects the city politically by making the governance framework visible and debatable before a controversy — not in response to one
  • Establishes Cleveland as a national model for accountable public safety technology deployment — a positive civic narrative rather than a defensive one
  • Gives Council members a concrete framework to defend to constituents who have surveillance concerns
Strategic Outcome
The governance compact isn't a concession to critics — it's the smartest long-term protection for the surveillance tools CDP needs. Accountability infrastructure is what makes public safety technology politically sustainable over time. Without it, every new camera contract becomes a Council fight. With it, the conversation shifts from "should we have cameras" to "are the cameras being used the way we agreed." That's a debate Cleveland can win.
