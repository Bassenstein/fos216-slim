The Four Intelligence Layers That Enable Prediction

Data Source: CPD Data Center — 5+ years of CAD incidents, RMS reports, LPR reads, ShotSpotter events, arrest records geo-tagged to block level.

Prediction: Day-of-week, time-of-day, and location crime probability maps. The foundation all other layers build on. Mayor Bibb already proved this works — his 5-hotspot plan reduced murders 37% using manual historical analysis. The stack does this automatically, at block resolution, updated continuously.
What Gets Predicted
Where crimes are most likely to occur in next 6–24 hours

Layer 2
Real-Time Sensor
Data Source: Motorola CommandCentral live feeds — ShotSpotter GSR, LPR reads, 911 call density, unit GPS positions, camera analytics, radio emergency buttons.

Prediction: Real-time anomaly detection layered on top of historical baselines. A ShotSpotter event at 11pm in a predicted hotspot triggers a completely different response than the same event at 2pm in a low-risk area. Context converts sensors into intelligence.
What Gets Predicted
Escalation likelihood from ShotSpotter cluster patterns
Vehicle of interest movement trajectory from LPR sequence
Officer safety risk zones from real-time call volume spikes
Secondary incident likelihood from first incident location
Layer 3
Identity Intelligence
Data Source: Reveal Identifi — geo-tagged field contacts, biometric matches, watchlist hits, ID scan records — correlated with PremierOne RMS criminal histories.

Prediction: Known offender location data creates a proactive intelligence layer. When Identifi logs a person with a violent history in a predicted hotspot at 11pm on a Friday, that contact isn't just documentation — it's a pre-crime signal that the Data Center AI can correlate against prior incident patterns for that individual.
What Gets Predicted
Known high-risk individuals in predicted crime zones
Gang network presence patterns before incidents
Repeat offender location clustering ahead of events
Warrant hits from routine enforcement actions in hotspots
Layer 4
Terrain Intelligence
Data Source: Reveal Farsight — 3D terrain models of high-crime blocks, alleys, building complexes, and event venues stored in CPD Data Center, refreshed by drone overflights.

Prediction: Terrain intelligence enables predictive positioning — not just where crime will occur, but exactly where officers should be before it occurs to maximize both deterrence and response speed. A SWAT team positioned in a blind spot of a known drug stash house because Farsight said so is the definition of predictive policing.

Section 06 · Honest Assessment — Risks & Guardrails

What the Evidence Cautions and How CPD Mitigates It

Predictive policing has a documented track record of benefits — and a documented track record of failures. This is not technology that deploys without guardrails. CPD's DOJ consent decree actually creates a governance framework that, if applied thoughtfully, can make Cleveland's version of predictive policing better than what failed in Los Angeles and Chicago.

High Risk — Must Address
Algorithmic Bias & Feedback Loops
Historical crime data in Cleveland reflects decades of over-policing in specific East Side neighborhoods. An AI trained purely on arrest data will designate those same areas as high-risk — increasing patrol, which increases arrests, which feeds the model. LAPD's PredPol was suspended precisely for this. Chicago's "Heat List" was abandoned after discrimination accusations.
CPD Mitigation: Train models on crime victimization reports, not arrests. Require human analyst review of all AI recommendations before deployment. Conduct quarterly bias audits. Include community crime reporting data as a balancing signal. DOJ monitor reviews model methodology annually.
High Risk — Must Address
Over-Policing Concentrated Communities
Predictive systems that generate constant hotspot alerts for specific blocks risk turning those communities into zones of constant police pressure — eroding trust, creating legal liability, and potentially violating civil rights. The DOJ consent decree was triggered precisely by CPD's history of disproportionate enforcement.
CPD Mitigation: Hotspot deployment must include community services co-deployment (Bibb's model — violence interrupters, social services, employment referrals). Technology authorizes presence, not enforcement actions. Consent decree monitoring includes review of technology-driven deployment decisions.
Medium Risk — Manage Actively
Biometric Accuracy and False Positives
Facial recognition systems have documented higher error rates for darker-skinned individuals. A false Identifi match leading to a wrongful stop or arrest in Cleveland's consent decree environment creates major legal and political exposure. The technology must be used as an investigative aid, not a standalone basis for arrest.
CPD Mitigation: Identifi match is investigative lead only — not probable cause. Requires corroborating identification before action. All facial recognition use logged and reviewed. Minimum confidence threshold policy. Officers trained on false positive rates and required to document override decisions.
Medium Risk — Manage Actively
Data Security and CJIS Exposure
A data center storing biometric records, criminal histories, and 500TB of video is an extraordinarily high-value target for ransomware and nation-state actors. A breach of CPD's Identifi database would be a catastrophic civil liberties event. Cleveland's municipal IT infrastructure has not historically been hardened to this standard.
CPD Mitigation: CJIS Security Policy v6.0 compliance is mandatory, not optional. Zero-trust architecture, 24/7 SOC, air-gapped biometric data segment, annual penetration testing, FBI CJIS audit cycle. DC staff background checks more rigorous than for sworn officers.
Manageable — Governance Handles
Transparency and Community Trust
Cleveland communities — particularly on the East Side — have legitimate concerns about surveillance technology given the DOJ consent decree history. Deploying this stack without public engagement could trigger political opposition that derails the program before it produces results.
CPD Mitigation: Mayor Bibb's transparent hotspot model (publicly announced, explained, and reported on) is a template. Publish technology deployment policy before launch. Community advisory board reviews AI model methodology. Annual public reporting on outcomes vs. deployment data. Privacy impact assessment published.
Manageable — Governance Handles
Technology Doesn't Replace Root Causes
McKinsey estimates AI can reduce urban crime 30–40%, but no technology eliminates crime driven by poverty, unemployment, disinvestment, and social fragmentation. CPD leadership must be clear internally and publicly that this is a force multiplier — not a replacement for community investment.
CPD Mitigation: Bibb's 2024 model explicitly paired hotspot enforcement with violence interrupters, employment referrals, and social services. The RTCC should have a community liaison role that translates intelligence into social service deployment alongside law enforcement response.
